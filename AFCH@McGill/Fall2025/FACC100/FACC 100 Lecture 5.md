As our company has definitely provided Company A with batteries that will not meet the agreed specifications, the ethical dilemma becomes at what level to accept the responsibility, and what is the correct way to respond to the mistake. Here the option of trying to hide the issue, as was so important to the ethics of LeMessurier, is not viable, because of the fact that it is inherent to shipped products.

I will consider several courses of action, notably: blaming the Engineer In Training, blaming the engineer, and blaming no single employee, as well as internal changes like firings or restructurings. When examined using Mill's theory of utilitarianism, a large contributor towards maximizing total benefit is in the acknowledgment of the issue, and taking effective steps to resolve it, as allowing the issue to remain would be detrimental to the customers and reputation of Company A. Mill's theory of utilitarianism would also seek to limit the potential harm to the reputation of EV+, which requires a careful balance between taking responsibility for the issue, and reassuring the public of the steps being taken to address it.

Kant's formalism would suggest that someone must have violated their duty to act ethically in order for the issue to arise, as the outcome is unethical, however it must be noted that all individuals were acting with good will, just under constraints. When examining the potential courses of action, the individual consequences are less of a concern than good will in the resolution, which would indicate a solution involving some systemic change, and less individual blame/harm to be the preferred option. Aristotle's virtue ethics' focus on logic and deduction, would indicate that the decision from the EIT to use ChatGPT instead of admitting that they could not accomplish the work, was an illogical decision, as the EIT could have known that it would not produce quality work. However, when applying virtue ethics to the potential solutions, instead of using it to determine blame, the focus on reason highlights a key aspect of the situation. The heart of this problem was not the EIT's decision to use ChatGPT, but rather the time pressure that the EIT (and the engineer) faced preventing them from producing good work, thus the most logical solution would be one that addresses that.

It is also important to consider the legal nature of this issue in determining fault, as the licensed engineer signed off on the work, they are likely responsible for it, and arguably should bear the consequences.
