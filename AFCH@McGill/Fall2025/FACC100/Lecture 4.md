The challenges & decisions LeMessurier faced as described by the Morgenstern article mainly include the technical challenges of undertaking the repairs, as well as the decisions about when and what to tell the public relating to the building's safety, and how that would impact LeMessurier's reputation. This differs from the Kremer article, which raises concerns about the lack of communication, and how the incident was covered up. This article discusses the evacuation plans, but raises concern over the level of danger that LeMessurier's desire to keep this secret posed to a large section of the nearby public. These articles differ as Morgenstern avoids the notion of blame, almost entirely absolving LeMessurier, while Kremer takes a more critical stance, both criticizing LeMessurier's unethical and unwise decisions, especially focusing on his efforts to hide the events from the public.
In LeMessurier's position I would have also brought the concerns about the building forward, despite the risk for backlash or litigation. The sheer scale of the danger this building poses, which LeMessurier made sure he understood, would be a disaster if permitted to be occur through inaction. I believe that here inaction is much the same as being actually responsible, and it would be very hard to treat ignoring the issue as an ethical option. This represents the professional responsibilities of the engineer as it demonstrates a commitment to the public (who would be endangered without action), as well as ethical conduct, but being willing to take the risk of personal backlash.
The decision to neglect quartering winds initially was absolutely a mistake. Engineers should go above and beyond of the code requirements, especially when lives are at stake. This situation especially warrants extra care, because the CitiCorp tower is likely a very different style of building than the code writers had intended, meaning it presents new challenges. LeMessurier's supervision of the repair work was adequate in my opinion, and I fully support the decision to bring in additional firms to conduct analyses as that reduces the likelihood that anyone has made a calculation error.
